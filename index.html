What this activity can and cannot tell you

A Jungian reflection lens, not a measurement tool

Analytical psychology uses “shadow” for parts of experience not integrated into conscious identity. That framework does not make these six answer directions validated archetypes. The result is a transparent count of your own choices.

Self-Awareness Boundary: This custom activity is an educational self-reflection prompt. It is not a clinical psychological evaluation, diagnosis, or therapy substitute.

Six Behavioral Response Directions

  • Appease: Suppressing personal frustration to avoid conflict and maintain external harmony.
  • Perfect: Demanding flawlessness and harsh self-criticism to prevent future vulnerability.
  • Control: Organizing variables and seeking the upper hand to guard against feeling powerless.
  • Withdraw: Emotional retreat and avoidance to escape interpersonal overwhelm.
  • Assert: Transparently naming boundaries and communicating authentic feelings.
  • Caretake: Over-focusing on others' emotional comfort while neglecting personal needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the shadow in Carl Jung's psychology?

A: The shadow contains aspects of oneself that have been repressed or disowned. Shadow work brings unconscious reactions into awareness for healthy integration.

Q: Does this quiz measure clinical traits or disorders?

A: No. It is a recreational self-reflection prompt counting eight conscious choices, not a diagnostic or psychological instrument.

Q: Are my choices or pattern scores saved on external servers?

A: Never. All calculations are executed completely inside your browser runtime. No answers, counts, or pattern names are stored externally.
